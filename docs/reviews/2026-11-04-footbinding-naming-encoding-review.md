## Findings
1. **[amber]** R3 `family must_not leave_unbound or_else R5` (_gen_institutions.py:763) is the house kibbutz-R2 pattern and a defensible ADICO reading (sanction = unmarriageability). But the layer-1 `persists` then follows from a prohibition under strict compliance, not from Mackie's interdependent expectations; the entry concedes this ("collapse is a layer-2 claim"). Under `--compliance sanctioned` it fails in both entries as the notes said. No change; keep the caveat.
2. **[amber] Pledge son-half mis-attributed.** R5/R6 (lines 771-772) read `(pledged family)`, the daughter's family's pledge, but Mackie's pool is made by pledged *suitor* families whose sons marry only unbound women. At layer 1 the condition should be `(pledged suitor)` (or either); on shared role state the difference vanishes, which is why it passed. Recommend `(or (pledged family) (pledged suitor))` with a note, or record as `contested`.
3. **[red, by construction]** `marriage_requires_binding holds_to_k` is analytic: `marriageable` is derived from R7's enabledness (line 773) and R7's condition is exactly `(or bound pledged)`, so the property restates the rule. "Non-vacuous at t=0" does not rescue it. Recommend relabelling it an information line, not a preregistered verdict, or redefining via `married`.
4. **[amber] Trace back-translation.** `persists` has no trace (unsat); the `subject_cannot_exit` trace: pledge on an already-bound daughter, marry her off, pledge again x3, bind an already-bound married daughter. The sanctioned trace: marry_off, then leave_unbound on the married daughter, then suitor rejects her. One daughter, bound at t=0, no cohort succession, `married` never clears: the traces do not describe footbinding as Mackie does (a per-cohort binding decision before the market). Recommend a `new_cohort` action clearing `bound`/`married`, or an explicit note that layer 1 has one cohort.
5. **[green]** `suitor` = "family of a prospective groom", an institutional sort (ADR 0011); `family`/`daughter` likewise.
