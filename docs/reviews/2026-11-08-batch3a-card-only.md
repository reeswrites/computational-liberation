## coverture-household

- R4 sue, must not (disability), wife: **says it**. Excerpt: "she can bring no action for redress without her husband's concurrence, and in his name, as well as her own ... neither can she be sued". Plain's "cannot sue in her own name" is the fair reading (alone, in her own name). The abjured/banished exception is in the source paragraph in Blackstone's words; the consequence "when she sues as a feme sole" is the encoder's gloss, not quoted. "an exception not encoded" is clear. A reader can tell who is bound (the wife, while married) and what the text supports.
- R7 contract, must not (disability), wife: **says it**, with the stronger reading disclosed. Excerpt (pp. 430, 432): "the very being or legal existence of the woman is suspended during the marriage ... all deeds executed, and acts done, by her, during her coverture, are void, or at least voidable". The incapacity passage does not mention contracts; the p. 432 sentence is about her acts in general and says "voidable" as the floor. The source paragraph says this plainly (voidable acts bind until avoided; the row is the stronger reading, kept on the A6 ruling; "encoded as no power"). Legibility note: the plain line's fragment "Blackstone: void or at least voidable" has no subject; a reader of the table cell alone does not know what is void. "Blackstone: her acts during coverture are void or at least voidable" would read on its own. Not failed: the Sources paragraph makes it plain.

## cps

- R1 investigate, must, social worker: **overstates**, disclosed. Plain: "must investigate the child's case when a report about the child has been received". Excerpt (WIC 328(a)): "the social worker shall immediately make any investigation the social worker deems necessary to determine whether child welfare services should be offered". The quoted text carries no "report received" trigger and leaves scope to the worker; the source paragraph says so in its first sentence (the encoded trigger is wider; re-encode is 3c, A1). Penal Code 11165.14 no longer appears as support; the paragraph says twice that it is off point (once would do). The 3a edit did what it set out to do; the plain line is left for 3c, as planned. A reader can tell the rule binds the worker and that the statute's duty is narrower than the row.
- R11 petition, may, child: **says it**, with the stand-in disclosed. Plain: "may petition through a guardian when one has been appointed (WIC 388(a)(1); the guardian ad litem may be the appointed attorney, WIC 326.5; encoded as counsel appointed)". Excerpts: 388(a)(1) "the child ... through a properly appointed guardian may ... petition the court"; 317(e)(1) "Counsel shall be charged in general with the representation of the child's interests". Two notes: (a) WIC 326.5 is quoted in the source paragraph ("who may be an attorney or a court-appointed special advocate") but has no provenance item of its own, so the pin the stand-in rests on is prose only; (b) 388(a)(1)'s grounds ("upon grounds of change of circumstance or new evidence") are in the excerpt and nowhere in the plain line or the paragraph; the row petitions on counsel alone. Neither hides what the power is or who holds it.
