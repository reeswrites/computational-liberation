# Handoff: Disability as a test case for computational-liberation

## Context
Repos: github.com/reeswrites/computational-liberation (theory, plan,
claims register, reviews, institutions dataset) and
github.com/reeswrites/philosophy-engine (generic engine).
Related Notion note: "Reinterpreting Abolition As Unbundling"
(pattern: abolition = unbundling; institutions bundle care with control;
keep the care, drop the coercion).

This thread explored disability liberation: how it overlaps with family
abolition (family as caregiver of last resort, care webs, "kith not kin")
and goes beyond it (institutions, guardianship, benefits rules, subminimum
wage, built environment, eugenics, psychiatric coercion). Then embryo
testing: screening offers less control than it seems because most
disability is acquired over a lifetime, so it can't substitute for support
systems. Key frame: reproductive choice is currently bundled with economic
fear; unbundle by funding the floor first.

## Proposed changes to the project
1. Life-course scoring: score each simulated person across a whole life
   with rising probability of illness/injury/aging, not a snapshot.
2. Freedom metric: run two definitions in parallel — "independence" vs.
   capability approach (Sen/Nussbaum: what a person can do and be, with
   support counted as enabling). Test which rediscovers known findings.
3. Freedom-vs-flourishing test: encode embryo screening as a case where
   parental reproductive freedom may conflict with disabled people's
   flourishing (expressivist objection). This tests an existing falsifier.
4. Floor-first sequencing claim: guaranteed support should make screening
   decisions less driven by economic fear. Needs data comparing screening
   and termination patterns across places with different support systems
   before entering the claims register.
5. Downstream feedback loops: (a) fewer people with a condition → fewer
   advocates, less research, thinner services; (b) curb-cut effect —
   accessibility built for some benefits nearly everyone.
6. Mechanism mapping: material (funding care/benefits), coordination
   (care webs, supported decision-making), imagination (social model of
   disability).

## New institutions to encode (each as a bundle: what's tied to what)
- SSI/Medicaid asset limits and marriage rules (support + poverty +
  marital status)
- Guardianship/conservatorship vs. supported decision-making (help + loss
  of legal rights)
- Institutional care vs. home/community care (Olmstead v. L.C., 1999)
- FLSA Section 14(c) sheltered work (job + segregation + subminimum pay)
- Embryo screening regimes across countries
